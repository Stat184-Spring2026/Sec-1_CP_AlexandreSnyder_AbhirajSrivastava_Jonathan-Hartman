# General Social SUrvey (GSS) Data Exploration

This repository explores GSS Data from years 2018 through 2023, with the context of the COVID-19 pandemic in mind, focusing on a few key variables such as confidence in institutions and other social factors.

## Overview

This repository serves as the representation of our skills surrounding the R language and supplementary software. We will be exploring the GSS Data from the years 2018 through 2023 about multiple variables about confidence in an array of institutes such as the executive branch of government, healthcare, and organized religion. 
Using these variables, we are hoping to gather insights about the widespread effects that the pandemic had on our society, represented by the general happiness and frequency of prayer variables. 
Our findings are that our variables generally didn't change as we expected them to with the pandemic in mind. Every variable had a small upward trend as years went by.

### Interesting Insight

An interesting insight that we had was that the COVID-19 pandemic did not majorly affect our sampled variables from the GSS Survey. We expected the pandemic to have a widespread social impact on the US, which it did, but our data doesn't seem to support that. This could be due to multiple outside factors confounding our research such as sample bias as it was a survey and we only had people who wilingly answered the survey as cases.

## Data Sources and Acknowledgements

Our data is sourced from The General Social Survey (GSS), which is a project of the independent research organization NORC at the University of Chicago, with principal funding from the National Science Foundation.

We extracted the variables frequency prayer, general happiness, confidence in organized religion, confidence in executive branch of government, confidence in health care systems, and the year these questions were asked using the GSS Data Explorer and Extractor.

We also made use of multiple Open Source R packages, such as tidyverse, ggplot2, etc.
